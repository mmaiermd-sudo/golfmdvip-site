Meet the crew

The people behind Golden Helmets & Green Jackets — on the mic and behind the scenes.

Michael Maier, M.D. “GolfDoc”

Host · Founder

Michael Maier, M.D. — “GolfDoc” to the show — is an orthopedic surgeon and has been an Assistant Professor of Orthopedic Surgery at Baylor College of Medicine since January 2018. A cum laude graduate of the University of Notre Dame (Class of 1994), he earned his M.D. at UT Southwestern Medical School and trained in general and orthopaedic surgery at Duke University Medical Center. Board certified by the American Board of Orthopaedic Surgery, he cares for athletes and active patients across sports medicine, joint reconstruction, trauma and golf-specific injuries, and holds Titleist Performance Institute Medical Level 3 certification. On Golden Helmets & Green Jackets he brings the operating-room view to college football, golf and the Notre Dame stories he has loved since his days under the Golden Dome. Go Irish!

Brandon Varon

Production Assistant

Brandon helps bring Golden Helmets & Green Jackets to life behind the scenes, supporting production of the episodes and Shorts and helping the show reach more fans every week. On the weekly picks board, his call is the Gutsy Pick of the Week.

Cade Cruzen

Production Assistant · Senior College Football Analyst, GolfDoc’s Weekly Futures

Market outlook: bullish on the Sooners. Cade Cruzen, a University of Oklahoma alum based in Dallas–Fort Worth, covers the SEC for the GolfDoc’s Weekly Futures desk, issuing buy, sell and hold ratings on every weekly slate. An Oklahoma die-hard who lives for Red River weekend and Matty Ice’s former fraternity brother, he goes head-to-head with GolfDoc and Brandon on the picks board, and he’s the analyst GolfDoc calls after a rough week.

His famous weekly three-pick parlay is Cade’s Turkey Triple, also called Cruzen’s Turkey Triple Play. It cashed at +567 in Week 8. Behind the scenes, he works with Brandon and the crew to bring Golden Helmets & Green Jackets to fans every week. Past performance is no guarantee of future results. Boomer Sooner, and gobble gobble.

Ricky Rodriguez

Chief Financial Officer & Accountant · Head College Football Futures Analyst

Ricky Rodriguez keeps the books for GolfDoc and Golden Helmets & Green Jackets as the show’s CPA, which makes him the one member of the crew who always knows the numbers. He runs Richard Rodriguez, CPA, PLLC by day and, on the weekends, chairs the GolfDoc’s Weekly Futures desk.

His research note, “Ricky’s Pearls,” delivers weekly against-the-spread positions, and he is not afraid to take the other side of GolfDoc’s book. Week 8 closed at 3–1–1 ATS, and for Week 9 he floated the “Row the Boat” moneyline underdog parlay (Northwestern, Wisconsin and Arizona), which he priced at +1,323. That three-team parlay’s nickname is Ricky’s Row the Boat. A Notre Dame grad and lifelong Irish fan, he believes in diversified portfolios and Golden Dome blue chips. Past performance is no guarantee of future results. Go Irish!

Coach Chris Morton

Offensive coordinator & quarterbacks coach · Klein High School

Coach Morton is Klein High’s offensive coordinator and quarterbacks coach, calling plays for the 2026 Bearkats, who won Klein’s first district title in 31 years. He grew up in the Klein system, was coached at Kleb by Gregg Blanchard, played for the Bearkats and went on to Texas Tech.

He came back to Klein as a coach in 2017, working with the tight ends and offensive line, then spent several seasons as offensive line coach at Fulshear, where he learned the wide-zone offense and helped build a playoff program. He returned home to Klein, moved from the offensive line to calling the offense, and is the voice behind the program’s rallying cry: the Original, the Mothership.

Troy Andrew

Group Product Manager of Trauma, Arthrex · Klein Class of 1996

Troy Andrew played on Klein’s 1995 district championship team, graduated with the Class of 1996 and went on to Duke, where he was a team captain. He signed with the Miami Dolphins as an undrafted rookie free agent, made the team and played alongside Zach Thomas and Ricky Williams, finishing with three years in the NFL and two in Europe.

After football he moved into orthopaedic sales, which is how he reconnected with GolfDoc, including time at Smith & Nephew. Today he is Group Product Manager of Trauma at Arthrex in Naples, Florida.

Coach Daniel Shorter

Strength & conditioning coach · Assistant football coach · Klein High School

Coach Shorter is a Klein High graduate who did four years of ROTC at Klein, then served in the U.S. Air Force, where semi-pro football and strength training sparked his path into coaching. After Lone Star College and Sam Houston State, and a deployment overseas with the Air National Guard, he started teaching in Klein ISD.

He coached at Strack Intermediate for seven years, often against Coach Blanchard, then became head coach at Leaman Junior High, the feeder for Fulshear. He came back home to Klein for the 2026–27 school year to teach and coach, where he now coaches some of the same players he had in junior high, including quarterback Carter Lattelle.

Alex Huang

SMU men’s golf · Junior · Plano, Texas

Alex Huang is a junior on the SMU men’s golf team from Plano, Texas, and a Plano West graduate. He started playing golf in China before his family moved to Plano, then spent two seasons at North Texas, where he was a teammate of GolfDoc’s son Mitchell, before transferring to SMU this fall.

As a freshman in 2024–25 he played all 11 events and posted a team-best 73.48 stroke average. He tied for second at the Trinity Forest Invitational at 70-66-68–204, and his 66 there tied for the fifth-best single round in program history. He also tied for eighth at the AAC Championship. As a sophomore in 2025–26 he averaged 72.79, just .12 behind Mitchell for the team lead, with five top-20 finishes, a tie for third at the Compadres Collegiate and 121 birdies. He opened his SMU career with a top-10 finish at the ACU Intercollegiate.

A.J. McGunigal

Founder, ACT 2 Health · Notre Dame quarterback, 1996

A.J. McGunigal is the founder of ACT 2 Health, an Austin-based, clinician-led longevity and wellness practice focused on diagnostics, metabolic health, hormones and peptides. He played quarterback at Notre Dame, wearing No. 14 on the 1996 team, Lou Holtz’s final season, and he is part of Holtz’s Heroes, the brotherhood of former players helping each other age well.

GolfDoc and A.J. met at O’Rourke’s in South Bend on Notre Dame–Miami weekend. On Episode 15 he shared Lou Holtz stories and the meaning behind “Act 2,” and made the case for measuring before acting: “Let’s not go in blindly. Let’s get some testing done.”

Dr. Mark A. Mighell

Shoulder & elbow surgeon, Florida Orthopaedic Institute · U.S. Navy veteran

Dr. Mighell is a board-certified shoulder and elbow surgeon at Florida Orthopaedic Institute in Tampa, where he has practiced since 2001 and trains three shoulder and elbow fellows each year. His work includes reverse total shoulder replacement, shoulder reconstruction, elbow arthroscopy and robotically assisted shoulder arthroplasty. He is an associate professor of orthopaedic surgery at the University of South Florida and a national board examiner for the American Board of Orthopaedic Surgery.

A University of Virginia graduate who earned his M.D. at the University of Maryland, he served as a Navy medical officer, trained in undersea medicine and served in Bahrain during Operation Desert Storm. He then spent seven years on active duty in the Department of Orthopaedic Surgery at Naval Medical Center Portsmouth, earned a Navy Commendation and retired as a Commander. GolfDoc knows him through the Notre Dame Orthopaedic Society, where he spoke on shoulder robotics in 2026.

Dr. Brian L. Badman

Shoulder surgeon, Central Indiana Orthopedics · South Bend native

Dr. Badman is a board-certified, fellowship-trained shoulder surgeon at Central Indiana Orthopedics in Fishers and Zionsville, Indiana. He treats sports-related shoulder injuries and degenerative and traumatic shoulder conditions, including total and reverse shoulder replacement, arthroscopic rotator cuff and labral repair, biceps tenodesis and shoulder fracture care.

He grew up in Fighting Irish country in South Bend and earned his undergraduate and medical degrees at Indiana University. After his orthopaedic residency at the University of Florida, he completed a shoulder and elbow fellowship at Florida Orthopaedic Institute under Dr. Mighell and Dr. Mark Frankle. In 2014 he became the third surgeon in Indiana invited into the American Shoulder and Elbow Surgeons. He has practiced in the Indianapolis area since 2006, teaches residents as volunteer faculty at the IU School of Medicine, and is a proud Notre Dame dad.

From West Point to the show

John Robb

USMA Class of 1991 · Army linebacker

John grew up in Houston and didn’t know what West Point was until his junior year of high school, when Army coach Bob Sutton came to recruit him. He played linebacker for Jim Young and Bob Sutton and co-captained the 1990 Army team that beat Navy 30–20.

Today he is founder and managing director of The Stone Creek Group, a Denver wealth-management firm built on “Duty. Honor. Family.” He spent 2001–2015 at Bernstein, has served on the West Point Association of Graduates board and Investment Committee, and earned his MBA at Our Lady of the Lake University.

Otto Leone

USMA Class of 1991 · Former Army quarterback

Otto earned his engineering degree at West Point and quarterbacked Army from 1987 to 1990 in Jim Young’s wishbone. On Halloween 1987 he started the year as sixth-string QB, came in against Temple, and his only pass went 64 yards to Mark Charette for a touchdown in a 17–7 Army win.

He later earned his MBA at Notre Dame, spent 2009–2024 at Schindler Elevator, finishing as VP of the South Central region, and is now SVP, Multi-Family, at Repipe Specialists. His advice to West Point’s Class of 2029: “Be a great teammate, be a servant leader, take care of each other, and always put the mission first.”

Dr. Tom Parr

Dr. Thomas J. “Tom” Parr, M.D., F.A.C.S., is a 1967 West Point graduate and Army Ranger who commanded an armored cavalry troop in Germany and a mechanized infantry company in Vietnam, then became an orthopaedic surgeon and retired from the Army Medical Corps as a Colonel in 1987. He has practiced orthopaedics in Sugar Land for more than 30 years, now with Baylor St. Luke’s Medical Group, where he is GolfDoc’s partner, and has served on the board of the West Point Society of Greater Houston since 1989. Tom and his wife Joannie brought John Robb and Otto Leone into GolfDoc’s life. Episode 10 is dedicated to Tom, Joannie and Bill York.
